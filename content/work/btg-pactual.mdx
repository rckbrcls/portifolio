## Where the problem gets real

I work on foreign-exchange software where a feature rarely stops at a screen or
an endpoint. The interesting problems sit between product rules, integrations,
persistence, and validation, and they have to behave correctly under real
operational pressure.

That means the first version of a requirement is usually not ready to become
code. I have to understand what traders and domain experts actually need, where
the constraints come from, and what the system should do when the happy path
stops being happy.

## What I own end to end

I sit with the full path of a feature: shaping its behavior with the people who
own the domain, writing code across frontend and backend, validating the edge
cases, and helping take the change to production.

My official title is IT Assistant. I joined as a Software Engineer Intern in May
2023 and moved into this role in May 2025. Software engineering is still the
craft I practice every day, but I keep the official title clear.

## Turning domain rules into software

I spend a lot of time turning business behavior into rules the system can
enforce consistently. We discuss expected behavior early, expose the awkward
cases, and only then carry the decision through APIs, persistence, interfaces,
and automated validation.

I have taken an external integration and an FX workflow through that full path.
I have also helped move backend responsibilities toward clearer
service-oriented boundaries and worked with concurrent processing where timing
and validation need to agree.

## What careful delivery looks like

The visible outcome is software that reaches production with its rules made
explicit enough to review, test, and operate safely. That is less dramatic than
a single architecture reveal, but it is the part of the work I value most:
reducing ambiguity before it becomes an expensive mistake.

I cannot publish internal topology, counterparties, or operational metrics. The
useful claim I can stand behind is the engineering discipline behind what I
ship, not a number I cannot defend publicly.
